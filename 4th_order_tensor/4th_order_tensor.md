## Function Description

Given a three-dimensional vector together with two weighting coefficients, the procedure computes two independent scalar quantities generated by fixed tensors of different orders. One quantity is produced by a quadratic tensor contraction and the other by a fourth-order tensor contraction. The two quantities are then combined through a generalized harmonic-mean formula controlled by the input weights. The output is rounded to exactly four decimal places, where output equal to -1.0000 indicates singularity.


## Mathematical Formulation

Let $ x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ with $x_{i} \in [-5,5].$ Define the fixed order-2 tensors

$$
F = \begin{bmatrix}  9 & 9 & 10 \\
                     17 & 27 & 21 \\
                     4 & 7 & 27
    \end{bmatrix},

G = \begin{bmatrix}  3 & 1 & 4 \\
                     2 & 1 & 0 \\
                     2 & 2 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Although $F$ and $G$ are not symmetric, the observable quantities depend only on the quadratic forms generated by these tensors. The black-box function is fully recoverable from input-output behavior, but the individual asymmetric components of $F$ and $G$ are not uniquely identifiable.

(Note: The tensors coordinates are deterministic constants adopted from a cited source.)

### Path A: Quadratic Tensor Contraction
The first scalar quantity is

$$
f(x)=\langle F,x^{\otimes2}\rangle = \sum_{i,j = 1}^{3} F_{i,j} x_i x_j,
$$

This is a standard order-2 tensor contraction.

### Path B: Fourth-Order Tensor Contraction
The second scalar quantity is

$$
g(x) = \langle T,x^{\otimes4}\rangle = \sum_{i_1,…,i_4 = 1}^{3} T_{i_1,…,i_4} x_{i_1} … x_{i_4}.
$$

where, $T = G \otimes G$ . This is a nonlinear 4th-order tensor contraction.

### Merging Stage
The final `score` (rounded to 4 decimal places) is

$$H = \frac{2f(x)g(x)}{w_{1} f(x)+w_{2} g(x)},$$

where for $w_{1} = w_{2}=1$ it gives the classical harmonic mean, for $w_{1} = 2, w_{2} = 0$ and $g \neq 0$, the expression reduces to $H = g$; similarly, for $w_{1} = 0, w_{2}=2$ and $f \neq 0$, the expression reduces to $H = f$. If the denominator $w_{1} f + w_{2} g$ equals zero, the implementation returns $-1.0$.


## Inputs
* `x = (x_1,x_2,x_3)`: float $\in$ [-5.0,5.0]
* `w1`: float $\in$ [0.0,2.0]
* `w2`: float $\in$ [0.0,2.0]


## Outputs
* `score`: float rounded to exactly 4 decimal places


## Pseudocode

1. Define the fixed order-2 tensor (F).
2. Define the fixed order-4 tensor $T = G \otimes G$.
3. **Path A:** Compute $ f(x) = \langle F,x^{\otimes2}\rangle.$
4. **Path B:** Compute $ g(x) = \langle T,x^{\otimes4}\rangle.$
5. **Merging:** Compute $ H =\frac{2fg}{w_{1} f+w_{2} g}$ if $w_{1}f+w_{2}g \neq 0$, otherwise -1.0.
6. Return `score`$=\operatorname{round}(H,4)$.


## Assumptions

* The input vector belongs to $\mathbb R^3$.
* Tensor coefficients remain fixed throughout all evaluations.
* The denominator $ w_{1} f+w_{2} g$ is nonzero unless explicitly handled by the implementation.
* Floating-point arithmetic is deterministic.


## Limitations

* The task uses fixed tensors rather than arbitrary tensor inputs.
* The harmonic aggregation becomes singular whenever $w_{1}f + w_{2}g = 0.$
